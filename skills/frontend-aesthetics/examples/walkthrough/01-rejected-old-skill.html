See what light knows.

Tamber is a pocket spectrometer that reads the color of anything you point it at, down to the wavelength, and tells you what that color means.

Follow a reading Read the field guide

01

Aim

Hold Tamber two to three centimeters from the sample. The ring light around the aperture wakes when it senses something close, so you never press a button just to see where you are pointing.

Next: Capture

02

Capture

Press the side button once. Tamber takes a 0.4 second scan across 340 to 1000 nanometers, from near ultraviolet to near infrared, and repeats it three times to average out a shaky hand.

03

Watch the light split

A thin white line widens into a band running violet, blue, green, yellow, orange and red from left to right. The upper edge of the band forms a curve with a low point in the blue and a steep rise past the red, plotted from 340 to 1000 nanometers.

Violet to red, plotted against reflectance.

The reading arrives on your phone as a thin white line. The line fans out into its colors, violet on one side and deep red on the other, and then the top edge of that band settles into a curve: how much light the sample reflected at every wavelength.

04

Match

Tamber compares the curve against a library of 2,400 reference samples and shows the three closest matches. Each match is labeled Strong, Possible, or Weak, never as a percentage, because a percentage suggests more certainty than a field reading has.

Before your first reading

Calibrate once a day against the white tile in the case lid. Keep the aperture clean; a fingerprint shows up as a dip near 450 nm. Readings through glass or plastic are not reliable.
