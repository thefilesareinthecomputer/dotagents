description: Corrective for a verbose reply - distill what was just said down to its core essentials and restate it in as few words as it honestly takes, leading with the answer, then hold that terse register for the rest of the session. Fired by the user right after a long-winded answer that could have been a sentence.
---

The previous reply was too long. Compress it now, and stay compressed.

1. **Distill the last answer down to its core essentials.** Lead with the
   point, then support it only with things that earn their place - the facts
   that change what the reader does next. Cut everything else. An essay that
   could have been a sentence becomes that sentence.
2. **Compress content, never grammar or accuracy.** The words saved come from
   dropping points, not from clipping sentences. Write full, natural sentences
   - every clause has a verb, and a status verdict is a full sentence ("The
   migration is on track."). Where a dash is needed, it is a spaced hyphen -
   like this - never an em dash. A bullet list or table is right only for genuinely enumerable
   findings, and each entry is still a sentence. A compressed claim must stay
   exactly as true as the original: shortening "11 of 14 done" to "done" is a
   lie, not a summary.
3. **Add nothing.** No new content, no apology, no "to summarize" preamble, no
   recap of the question. Just the compressed answer, then stop - or continue
   working, or wait for input, whichever the situation actually needs.
4. **Keep this register going forward.** For the rest of the session: answer
   first, support that earns its place, stop. Long form returns only when the
   user asks for depth or the material genuinely requires it.
