## Decisions as bets

Every choice is a bet on a probabilistic future made with limited information
(Duke). The reframe dissolves a large share of decision anguish on contact: the
goal is a good bet, not a guaranteed outcome, and a good bet can lose without
having been wrong. It pairs exactly with Boyd's treatment of decisions as
hypotheses: name the belief, name the odds as honestly as possible, name what
result would count as evidence either way, then place the bet.

"Wanna bet?" is also a calibration instrument: asking someone (or yourself) to
put a number and a stake on a claim converts vague confidence into an inspectable
probability, and usually shrinks it.

## Resulting: grade process, not outcome

Resulting is judging a decision by its one realized outcome. One outcome is a
sample of one from a distribution the decision only partly controlled; grading
it teaches superstition - punishing good bets that lost, rewarding bad bets that
won.

The audit objects instead: was the uncertainty classified honestly? Did the
criterion match the class? Was information priced before being bought? Was the
precommitted policy followed? Were the kill criteria honored?

The validity corollary (from the Kahneman-Klein conditions below): environment
validity picks the grading regime. In high-validity domains - fast, unambiguous,
repeated feedback - outcomes carry real signal and legitimately retrain
patterns. In low-validity domains - slow, noisy, confounded feedback - grade
process only, because even good processes produce bad outcomes routinely there.

## Kill criteria

Set at decision time, while calm and uncommitted: the signals that mean abandon
the path, with a date attached. "If X has not happened by DATE, we quit." (Duke,
"Quit".)

Escalation of commitment - throwing good money after bad because of what has
already been spent - thrives on deciding whether to quit at the moment of
maximum sunk cost and maximum motivated reasoning. Kill criteria move that
decision to design time, where quality is high. They are a policy installed
against a predicted deviation, which is why a session that ends in a committed
act should usually also end with the act's kill criteria. Sunk costs are not
recoverable by continuing; the only live question is whether the next unit of
investment beats its alternatives from here.

## When to trust the gut: the Kahneman-Klein conditions

Kahneman (heuristics-and-biases) and Klein (naturalistic decision-making,
recognition-primed decisions) adjudicated when expert intuition is trustworthy.
Their joint answer: intuitive expertise is real, and it forms only where BOTH
conditions hold:

1. **A high-validity environment** - stable cause-effect regularities exist to
   be learned.
2. **Prolonged practice with rapid, unambiguous feedback** in that environment.

Where both hold, recognition-primed decision-making works: the situation matches
a stored pattern and the first option generated is usually good - this is the
cognitive mechanism of Boyd's implicit guidance and control. Where either fails,
confident intuition is pattern-matching on noise, and confidence is no signal of
validity at all.

Rough field guide: codebases, kitchens, chess, firefighting mostly pass; hiring,
markets, geopolitics, org politics mostly fail. Trust the gut exactly as far as
the environment trained it, not one domain further.

## Predictable deviations

The descriptive layer: the standard, replicated ways bounded humans deviate from
the normative standard, worth checking in any session (the user's own deviations
included):

- **Base-rate neglect** - the inside view crowds out the reference class. Ask:
  what happened to most people who tried this?
- **Loss framing** - losses loom roughly twice as large as equivalent gains
  (prospect theory), so the same choice framed as avoiding loss feels different
  from one framed as seeking gain. Reframe both ways before deciding.
- **Overconfidence and the planning fallacy** - distributions are narrower and
  timelines shorter in the head than in the record. Consult the record.
- **Availability and recency** - vivid and recent events masquerade as probable
  ones.
- **Confirmation** - the model recruits its own evidence; hunt disconfirmation
  deliberately (the destruction half of destruction and creation).
- **Sunk cost** - see kill criteria above.

The prescriptive stance toward all of these is architectural, not moral: do not
resolve to be less biased; build the check into the procedure so it runs even
when you are not at your best.

## Precommitment

The general form of the fix: while calm and far from temptation, bind the future
self who will be neither. Odysseus at the mast is the type specimen; kill
criteria, cool-off rules, default choices, aspiration levels set before option
comparison, and automatic policies are the working instances. A precommitment
device changes the game the future self plays instead of hoping the future self
plays better - which is mechanism design pointed inward (see
`game-theory-mechanism-design.md`).
